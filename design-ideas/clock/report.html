Coaching review: the game was over on move 11

Morphy vs. Duke of Brunswick & Count Isouard, Paris Opera House, 1858

Strength

Your pieces arrived before your attack did. From move 6 every piece went to its best square: Bc4 and Qb3 pointed at f7, Nc3 held the centre, and Bg5 pinned the knight before a single pawn moved on the kingside. When the sacrifices came, they were not gambles: they were the natural conclusion of a position you had already won.

Engine note · average centipawn loss 8.7, well inside the 1400 band's 40-point target.

Weakness

You recaptured with the piece doing the most work, twice. 5.Qxf3 pulled the queen into a pin line that didn't exist yet, and it cost you two tempi of development. The simpler habit: after every capture, ask which recapture leaves your best piece where it belongs.

You played

5.Qxf3: queen recaptures, development stalls

Engine line

5.gxf3: pawn recaptures, queen stays free to attack

Game Overview

A 17-move miniature decided by two removal sacrifices. Black never finished developing; your rooks reached the eighth rank first. Move 11 was the last moment Black could have defended. After that, every white move was a check or a capture.

Move 11: the check that forced the trade

Brilliant

Engine agrees

11.Bxb5+ Nbd7 12.O-O-O: the rook joins with tempo

Black had to block with a knight, so the rook that defends d7 never got a move of its own. That single check is why 13.Rxd7 works two moves later.

Move 13: removing the defender

Best

13.Rxd7: a rook for a knight, to tear the eighth rank open

Engine line

13.Rxd7 Rxd7 14.Rd1: doubled pressure down the d-file

Exchange sacrifices are not about the material count. Here the rook buys the d-file, and the d-file decides the game.

Move 16: the queen walks into capture

Count the defenders first: the d8 square had none left. That is the whole calculation. Then the queen sacrifice plays itself.

Ask about this game Coaching plan
You
Why did the engine call 5.Qxf3 an inaccuracy? The queen felt natural.
Coach
Natural is the problem: it is the move a developing player plays on autopilot. After 4...Bxf3, the queen on f3 blocks the f-pawn and your knight on b1 now needs three moves to reach the kingside. The pawn recapture 5.gxf3 keeps g2 free for the king and opens the g-file for your rook later. You still won here because Black never punished it. At 1600+ it gets punished, usually with ...d5 and ...Bc5 hitting f3 directly.
You
What should I drill this week?
Coach
Twenty minutes a day on sacrifice puzzles, starting with the links below the report. Before each one, say out loud which defender you are removing. The habit matters more than the rating of the puzzle set.
