Chessmasters: four design directions

UI exploration · October 2026
2 surfaces each: landing + sample review

Four complete visual worlds for the same product: paste a game, get a streamed coaching review. Each direction builds both the landing page (conversion) and the sample review (the working product): same content, same sample game, four different worlds. Open them side by side; the boards on this page are the same position rendered in each direction's palette.

The Two Clocks

Chess-clock hardware as the interface: pure white and black registers, hairline seams, one signal orange. Every evaluation appears as a pair of registers: your move against the engine's.

Built from mid-century tournament equipment: enamel-white and black fields, mechanical numerals, zero chrome. The signature move is the dual register: paired readouts for every claim, with the eval graph drawn as a barcode ribbon swept by a single orange hand.

The Bulletin

The great tournament bulletins of 1895, set for the screen: warm ebony ground, boxwood-cream serifs, ruled notation columns, letterpress red for the annotations.

The review becomes a printed dispatch: a Bodoni masthead, prose in Spectral, the score in Courier Prime, and margin notes in carmine. The signature move is the annotated score: the game's moves held in the margin with the coach's note pinned to each key move.

The Scorebook

Daylight, graph paper, and a red pencil. A coach at the board marking up your scoresheet: circles around the mistakes, arrows through the ideas, notes in the margin.

Archivo's condensed sports-poster voice on ruled paper; green-and-cream board; every finding carries a pencil annotation. The signature move is the coach's pencil: hand-drawn circles and arrows drawn onto the position, and margin notes in pencil red.

The Grandmaster's Study

The incumbent world, raised: the dark ink shell and antique gold you already have, pushed into a lamplit private study: brass rules, leather, parchment, engraved plates.

Keeps Chessmasters' existing DNA (Fraunces, DM Sans, gold on ink, the parchment report) and removes its weakest habits (no colored sidebar cards, no eyebrow labels), replacing them with letterpress frames, plate-numbered diagrams, and a reading experience built for long prose. The signature move is the plate and the pen: key positions framed as numbered engraved plates with brass annotation lines.

How to judge these

  • Open the landing and the sample review for one direction together: the world must hold from marketing page to working screen.
  • Read the review, don't just look at it: is the coaching easy to follow, and can you tell what to do next?
  • Ask who it is for: players 800–1800 who want plain language, not engine numbers. Which world would they trust with their games?
  • Everything here is a design concept: the sample game is public history, engine values are illustrative, and review quotes are placeholders.
