Coaching review: the game was over on move 11

Morphy vs. Duke of Brunswick & Count Isouard, Paris Opera House, 1858 · avg centipawn loss 8.7

Strength

Your pieces arrived before your attack did. From move 6 every piece went to its best square: Bc4 and Qb3 pointed at f7, Nc3 held the centre, and Bg5 pinned the knight before a single pawn moved on the kingside. When the sacrifices came, they were not gambles: they were the natural conclusion of a position you had already won.

engine note: average centipawn loss 8.7 against a 40-point target for your rating band

Weakness

You recaptured with the piece doing the most work, twice. 5.Qxf3 pulled the queen into a pin line that didn't exist yet, and it cost two tempi of development. The habit to build: after every capture, ask which recapture leaves your best piece where it belongs.

Margin note

5.Qxf3 vs 5.gxf3 — the pawn recapture keeps the queen free to attack and opens the g-file for the rook. You still won; at 1600+ this gets punished by ...d5 and ...Bc5 hitting f3.

Game Overview

A seventeen-move miniature decided by two removal sacrifices. Black never finished developing; your rooks reached the eighth rank first. Move 11 was the last moment Black could have defended. After that, every white move was a check or a capture.

Key moments

Moment 11 · after 10...cxb5

The check that forced the trade

The bishop drags the last kingside defender away with tempo. Black must block with a knight, so the rook guarding d7 never gets a move of its own — which is why 13.Rxd7 works two moves later.

Moment 13 · after 12...Rd8

Removing the defender

A rook for a knight to tear the eighth rank open. Exchange sacrifices are not about the material count: the rook buys the d-file, and the d-file decides the game.

Moment 16 · after 15...Nxd7

The queen walks into capture

16.Qb8+!! is the only mate in one: the queen is bait, and the back rank was already undefended. Count the defenders before the sacrifice: here, d8 had none left.

Focus Areas

Train next

Exchange sacrifices: give material to remove the one piece holding the position together. You found the idea here; the next step is trusting it when the material count looks bad for two more moves.

Then

Recapture discipline: choose the recapturing piece that keeps your best piece pointed at the king. 5.Qxf3 cost two tempi here.

Why did the engine call 5.Qxf3 an inaccuracy? The queen felt natural.
Coach
Natural is the problem: it is the move a developing player plays on autopilot. The queen on f3 blocks the f-pawn and your knight on b1 now needs three moves to reach the kingside. 5.gxf3 keeps g2 free and opens the g-file. You still won because Black never punished it; at 1600+ it gets punished, usually with ...d5 and ...Bc5 hitting f3 directly.
