The game was over on move 11

Morphy vs. Duke of Brunswick & Count Isouard, Paris Opera House, 1858

Strength

Your pieces arrived before your attack did. From move 6 every piece went to its best square: Bc4 and Qb3 pointed at f7, Nc3 held the centre, and Bg5 pinned the knight before a single pawn moved on the kingside. When the sacrifices came, they were not gambles: they were the natural conclusion of a position you had already won.

Engine note · average centipawn loss 8.7 against a 40-point target for your rating band.

Weakness

You recaptured with the piece doing the most work, twice. 5.Qxf3 pulled the queen into a pin line that didn't exist yet, and it cost two tempi of development. The habit to build: after every capture, ask which recapture leaves your best piece where it belongs.

In the margin

5.Qxf3 — the queen recaptures. 5.gxf3 keeps her free to attack and opens the g-file for the rook. You still won; at 1600+ this gets punished by ...d5 and ...Bc5 hitting f3.

Game Overview

A seventeen-move miniature decided by two removal sacrifices. Black never finished developing; your rooks reached the eighth rank first. Move 11 was the last moment Black could have defended. After that, every white move was a check or a capture.

Plate I · after 10...cxb5

The check that forced the trade

11.Bxb5+ drags the last kingside defender away with tempo. Black must block with a knight, so the rook defending d7 never gets , which is exactly why 13.Rxd7 works two moves later.

Removing the defender

13.Rxd7 gives a rook for a knight to tear the eighth rank open. Exchange sacrifices are not about the material count: the rook buys the d-file, and the d-file decides the game.

Focus Areas

Train next

Exchange sacrifices: give material to remove the one piece holding the position together. You found the idea here; the next step is trusting it when the material count looks bad for two more moves.

Then

The queen on f3 blocks the f-pawn and your knight on b1 now needs three moves to reach the kingside. 5.gxf3 keeps g2 free and opens the g-file. You still won because Black never punished it; at 1600+ it gets punished, usually with ...d5 and ...Bc5 hitting f3 directly.
